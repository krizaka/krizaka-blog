On Sunday 15 November at 1:24 pm Montréal time, the release of a krizaka-ui version stopped halfway. The step that runs every package's checks was cancelled after 3 minutes 35 seconds, with 95 of 99 tasks done. The log says one sentence: "The operation was canceled." The release before it had failed too, at 12:52 pm; the last one that worked was at 11:49 am.

The cockpit shows it on one line: a critical alert, raised by the watchdog, whose message starts with "main red: krizaka-ui release". It stayed open and was counted sixteen times between 2:15 pm and 6:00 pm.

This post is about that screen. It is a local app on my Mac that shows me the whole company in one place. I say why it exists, what we refused to do, what it cannot do yet, and then what seven different roles read on it. The captures are real screens, taken tonight; the sensitive values on them are masked, and I explain how at the end.

## The screen

One app, the cockpit, that runs on 127.0.0.1 and nowhere else. It is a Next.js app built with [our components](site:ui); it reads our local data and GitHub. It is not a dashboard for customers. It is for one person.

The left bar holds what matters to steer: « Aujourd'hui » (today), « La machine » (the machine), « Ce qui sort » (what ships) and costs. Further down, the company (identity, business, community, documents), then architecture. « La machine » has six tabs: health, delivery, quality, rules, automations, alerts.

What the first page of « La machine » shows tonight, each block with its own date:

- 11 team sessions at work;
- 4 red pull requests in the trains;
- 14 active scheduled tasks, all of which ran;
- 15 open alerts, 2 of them serious.

The banner on top says « En panne » (down): three blocks need a look. The cockpit does not reassure for the sake of it. When a block is too old, it says so on an orange chip (« Coûts cloud : vieux de 2 j », cloud costs: 2 days old).

## What made it necessary

The engine behind it, [krizaka-agents](repo:krizaka-agents), fits in one sentence: a team is launched from a brief, works isolated, has a cap in turns, spend and minutes, a watchdog watches it, a train lands only green pull requests, and a bug becomes a guard. [The architecture of all this](/basics/the-architecture-of-krizaka-agents/) is described elsewhere. Here the question is simpler: when ten sessions work at once, where does the person who decides look?

Without the cockpit, the answer would be: in several places. The sessions in the terminal, the trains in GitHub, the alerts in a database, the board in a browser. The cockpit puts them on one screen, each with its date.

## What we refused

- **A hosted service for our own operations.** The app only listens on the machine, has no remote repository and sends no document to anyone. Why: it reads the company folder (decisions, notes, documents), which is never pushed to GitHub, and the written rule is that no document's content goes to a third party. What it costs: it exists only on this Mac; I do not look at the company from my phone.
- **A local database that decides.** GitHub is the source: tickets, board, pull requests, required checks, merge queue. What is local is a copy, kept to display and to warn. The cockpit never writes to GitHub, neither the board nor the alerts; that goes through their tools. Why: GitHub enforces the required checks on every actor, us included. A local copy enforces nothing.
- **A model as judge.** The guards, the trains and the watchdog are ordinary code. A model can write a change; it never decides whether that change merges.
- **A machine that decides money, law or what cannot be undone.** A team has a cap on spend and time. What touches money beyond that cap, legal questions and irreversible actions go back to a person: the team writes it in its report under « Decisions » and carries on with the rest.

## One limit, honestly

The cockpit shows its data; it does not explain the cause. The release alert says that main is red, since when and how many times; it does not say why the runner cancelled the step. For that you open GitHub's log. And a figure has an age: tonight, two orange chips said « Coûts cloud : vieux de 2 j » and « Graphe de code : vieux de 2 j » (cloud costs and code graph, 2 days old). The cockpit says so; refreshing takes a gesture, a « Rafraîchir » button.

## What each role reads on it

One story for everyone, then a short part per role: what that person would read on the screen, and why it matters to them. We are not seven people; it is the same screen, read with seven questions.

**Site reliability.** The Alerts tab: one line per subject, with its severity, its source (the watchdog or a team session), how many times it came back and its state (open, acknowledged, resolved). A critical alert that returns sixteen times reads faster than a message that scrolls by: you see that a problem lasts, not only that it happened.

**Project direction.** « Ce qui sort »: per product, the board, the open pull requests, the state of the train, the last release, the last deployment. Each card carries the date of its oldest source. A project is judged on what actually left, not on what was planned.

**Product management.** The board, from epic to task, and « Aujourd'hui », which separates what waits for a human decision from the rest. Tonight, 33 urgent actions out of 82 on the board. What the agents cannot decide piles up there: it is the queue that limits the rest.

**Architecture.** The « Architecture » screen draws, in C1 and C2, what each repository declares in its own architecture folder, and counts the gaps against what main contains. Read at 8:28 am today: 5 repositories of 16 in step, 83 gaps. A diagram that is not compared with the code ends up telling something else.

**Marketing.** « Communauté »: who follows us, what moves people, and whether the publication calendar is kept. The figures come from each network's official interface, filed in the local database; nothing leaves the Mac. The screen also says when a sync never happened and what it lacks.

**Bookkeeping.** The cost and company screens are the ones we do not show: they are masked on every capture of this post, and they will stay so. What we can say: the person who keeps the books reads there what the company spends this month, line by line, with the date of the update.

**Running the company.** « Aujourd'hui »: what waits for me, the decisions to take, the next date. The first line says « 38 choses t'attendent. Le reste avance sans toi. » (38 things are waiting for you. The rest moves without you.) It is the sentence I wanted, and what the cockpit has to prove every day.

## How the captures are made

Blurring a value is not safe: a blur and a pixelation can be undone on text. So we never blur a real value. Before the capture, a script walks the page and replaces every sensitive value (amounts, e-mail addresses, numbers, keys, private paths, any block that talks about finance or security) with a neutral text of the same length, and blurs only that neutral text. Then a guard reads all the visible text of the page again, with wider filters than the masking used, and refuses to write the image if anything is left. The files are stripped of their metadata. The script and its tests are in the blog's repository.

One thing follows: what the pixels of a capture do not show, they do not show. There is nothing underneath to recover. That is also why the captions say « sensitive values masked ».
