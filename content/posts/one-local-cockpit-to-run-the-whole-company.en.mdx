On Sunday 15 November, at 1:24 pm Montréal time, the release of a krizaka-ui version stopped. The step that runs the checks of every package was cancelled after 3 minutes 35 seconds, with 95 tasks out of 99 done. The release before it had failed too, at 12:52; the last one that worked was at 11:49.

The cockpit showed it on one line: a critical alert, raised by the watchdog, whose message starts with « main red: krizaka-ui release ». It stayed open, counted sixteen times between 2:15 pm and 6 pm.

I did not do the next steps; the agent that pilots our teams did, Mouloud, the CTO session. It read the alert, then opened the full log on GitHub, which says more than the alert: the runner received a shutdown signal (« The runner has received a shutdown signal ») during the checks step. It was not the 45-minute time limit, and not two releases stepping on each other. Mouloud wrote the ticket and launched a team on it, from a brief: the team works isolated, with a ceiling on turns, spend and minutes, and a watchdog keeps an eye on it.

The team proved the cause. The release ran the build, the lint, the tests and the typecheck of the ui package all at once, with no cache. The build alone peaks at about 15 GB of memory, on a runner that has 16. The runner was shut down, and what the tests had printed (axe-core's « Frame does not have a content window », the notices of the simulated browser about iframes) was flushed only after the kill. The printed `window` object was a detail, not the cause. The fix runs the lint in two steps, caps the concurrency at 2 and silences those notices at the source; it was merged at 6:34 pm, the release that followed succeeded, version 4.9.0 is out, and the ticket is closed. The build's memory has its own follow-up ticket.

My part was to see it, on the cockpit, and to leave the agents to it. That is the point of the screen: a founder supervises a company of agents, he does not do each step.

This post describes the screen where I saw it. It is a local application, on my Mac, that shows me the whole company in one place. I say why it exists, what we refused to do, what it cannot do yet, then what seven different roles read there. The captures are real screens, taken on the evening of 15 November 2026; the sensitive values are masked, and I say how at the end.

## The screen

One application, the cockpit, that runs on 127.0.0.1 and nowhere else. It is a Next.js app built with [our components](site:ui); it reads our local data and GitHub. It is not a dashboard for customers, it serves one person.

In the left bar, everything that matters to run the place: « Today », « The machine », « What ships » and the costs. Further down, the company, then the architecture. Inside « The machine », six tabs: health, delivery, quality, rules, automations, alerts. On the evening of 15 November, the health page counted 13 team sessions at work, 4 red pull requests in the trains, 14 active scheduled tasks and 16 open alerts, 2 of them serious.

The cover of this post is the part that says what the company is doing right now: the section « Teams, trains and the work tree, in detail », opened. Each ticket in progress sits there under its feature and its epic, with its title, its state on the board and the pull requests that close it. A line whose epic is about security is blurred whole, like any block of that kind.

The cockpit does not lie to reassure. When a block is too old, it says so on an orange badge (« Cloud costs: 2 d old »), and refreshing it is a gesture, a button. That evening the « Delivery » block counted 196 pull requests merged in 24 hours. Nobody reads 196 notifications. One line turning red, you see.

## What made it necessary

The engine behind it, krizaka-agents, our product for running teams of agents, fits in one sentence: you launch a team from a brief, it works isolated, it has a ceiling on turns, spend and minutes, a watchdog keeps an eye on it, a train lands only the green pull requests, and a bug becomes a guard. [The architecture of all this](/basics/the-architecture-of-krizaka-agents/) is described elsewhere. Here the question is simpler: when ten sessions work at once, where does the person who decides look?

Without the cockpit, the answer would be: in several places. The sessions in the terminal, the trains in GitHub, the alerts in a database, the board in a browser. The cockpit puts them on one screen, each with its date.

## What we refused

An online service for our own operations. The application listens only on the machine, has no remote repository and sends no document to anyone. It reads the company's folder (decisions, notes, documents), which is never pushed to GitHub, and the written rule is that no document content goes to a third party. What it costs: it exists only on this Mac, and I do not look at the company from my phone.

A local database that decides. GitHub is the source: tickets, board, pull requests, required checks, merge queue. What is local is a copy to display and to warn, and the cockpit never writes to GitHub; that goes through their tools. GitHub enforces the required checks on every actor, us included. A local copy enforces nothing.

A model to judge. The guards, the trains and the watchdog are ordinary code. A model may write a change; it never decides whether that change merges.

A machine that decides about money, law or what cannot be undone. A team has a ceiling on spend and time. What touches money beyond that ceiling, legal questions and irreversible acts go back to a person: the team writes it in its report, under « Decisions », and carries on with the rest.

## One limit, honestly

The cockpit shows its data, it does not explain its cause. The release alert says main is red, since when and how many times; why is in GitHub's full log, where the CTO session found the sentence about the shutdown signal. And a figure has an age: that evening two orange badges said « Cloud costs: 2 d old » and « Code graph: 2 d old ». The cockpit says it; refreshing takes a gesture, and a whole collection takes something like fifteen seconds.

## What each role reads there

One story for everyone, then a short part per role: what that person would read on the screen, and why it matters to them. We do not have seven people; it is the same screen, read with seven questions.

For reliability, it is the « Alerts » tab: one line per subject, with its severity, its source (the watchdog or a team session), how many times it came back and its state. A critical alert that returns sixteen times reads faster than a message that passes: you see that a problem lasts. The rest is a loop, which that evening's alert went through: the alert becomes a ticket, the ticket a team, the team a pull request that the train lands if it is green. Each link has a ceiling: turns, spend, minutes, and a load brake. That evening, a launch of a team by the CTO session was refused because the Mac's five-minute load average was 86, above the brake of 60; the launch waited for the load to fall. It is a limit the machine puts on itself.

For project management, « What ships »: per product, the board, the open pull requests, the train's state, the last version, the last deployment. Each card carries the date of its oldest source. A project is judged by what actually left, not by what was planned.

For product management, the board, from epic to task, and « Today », which separates what waits for a human decision from the rest. What the agents cannot settle piles up there: it is the queue that limits the rest.

For architecture, the « Architecture » screen draws, in C1 and C2, what each repository declares in its own architecture folder, and counts the gaps with what main contains. A diagram that is not compared with the code ends up telling something else.

For marketing, « Community »: who follows us, what moves people, and whether the publishing calendar is kept. The figures come from each network's official interface, filed in the local database; nothing leaves the Mac. The screen also says when a sync has never happened and what it lacks.

For the person who keeps the books, the cost and company screens are the ones we do not show, and they stay masked. What that person reads there, with no amount here: the book is kept by us, in date order. One date rule splits it, 8 October 2026: before that day, spending is start-up cost, with no sales taxes; from that day, it is operating cost, with input tax credits. Receipts are filed from the inbox under one name pattern, duplicates are deleted, and private documents stay locked.

For running the company, « Today »: what waits for me, the decisions to make, the next deadline. The first line says « 38 things are waiting for you. The rest moves without you. » It is the sentence I wanted, and it is what the cockpit has to prove every day.

## How the captures are made

Blurring a value is not safe: a blur and a pixelation can be undone on text. So we never blur a real value. Before the capture, a script walks the page and replaces each sensitive value (amounts, e-mail addresses, numbers, keys, private paths, any block that talks about finance or security) with a neutral text of the same length, and blurs only that neutral text. Then a guard reads all the visible text of the page again, with wider filters than the masking, and refuses to write the image if anything is left. The refresh film follows the same rule, frame by frame. The files are stripped of their metadata. The script and its tests are in the blog's repository.

One gap remains: what the pixels of a capture do not show, they do not show. There is no underneath to recover. That is also why the captions say « sensitive values masked ».
