The usual story about generative AI failing is that people prompt badly: type six words, get a monster with three legs, blame the model. We set out to show that story with pictures. What we found is more useful and less tidy, so this post shows what we actually got, with the prompt, the seed and the time under every picture, and says where the story did not hold.

<Note>
Every picture here was made today on one MacBook Pro (Apple M1 Max, 64 GB) with the engine's command line, **one image per prompt, one seed (2026)**. That is a demonstration, not a benchmark: no scoring, no repetition across seeds. We say so again at the end.
</Note>

## Level 0: the terse prompt

The prompt: *a basketball player dunking in snow*. Nine words, the kind a user types.

<Figure id="anatomy-flux-l0" alt="A basketball player in a navy jersey dunking in a snowy park, a hillside with trees behind" caption="Level 0, FLUX.2 [klein] 4B. Two arms, two legs, a rim and a net. The jersey lettering is gibberish." />

This is not the anatomical nightmare the usual story promises. FLUX.2 [klein] 4B, an open model released with a permissive licence, produced a plausible dunk in snow from nine words. What the prompt left open, the model decided: a navy jersey, a snowy hillside, no particular camera. What it got wrong is small and typical: the letters on the jersey read as nothing.

## Levels 1 to 3: adding what you mean

Each level adds one family of detail to the one before. Same model, same seed, same size, 77 to 82 seconds each.

<Row>
<Figure id="anatomy-flux-l1" alt="A basketball player in a red jersey leaping toward a rim inside an indoor arena" caption="Level 1: + who and how. Red jersey, both legs visible, ball above the hoop." />
<Figure id="anatomy-flux-l2" alt="A basketball player in a red jersey dunking on an outdoor asphalt court dusted with snow" caption="Level 2: + where. An outdoor court, fresh snow, falling flakes." />
<Figure id="anatomy-flux-l3" alt="A red-jersey player dunking on a snowy court, overcast light, the background softly out of focus" caption="Level 3: + camera and light. A medium shot, 35 mm, f/2.2, soft overcast daylight." />
</Row>

What changed, as we see it:

- **Level 1 fixed the jersey and lost the snow.** We described the player and said nothing of the place, so the model filled the gap with a lit indoor arena. A prompt is a set of constraints, and a dimension you leave free is decided for you. Adding detail on one axis can displace what you had before.
- **Level 2 brought the snow back** and put the court outdoors, because we now said so.
- **Level 3 changed the frame and softened the background; the content stayed the same.** It did not fix the lettering on the jersey, and the backboard has an odd second panel under the first. The light was already overcast at Level 2, since the scene was snow, so we cannot credit the lighting words with a change.

The full prompts of the four levels are under each picture (open **How this was made**). Read in order they are the usual recipe: subject and anatomy, setting and physics, camera and optics.

## The negative prompt: a tool one of these models does not have

The fourth step of the usual recipe is a negative prompt (*deformed, extra limbs, bad anatomy, glitch, jitter, low quality*). FLUX.2 [klein] runs at guidance 1 and ignores a negative prompt; [Orazaka's Studios](https://dev.orazaka.com) say so and keep "no text, no logos" in the positive prompt instead (ADR-075). So the negative step cannot be shown on this model, and we do not pretend to.

It can be shown on Stable Diffusion 1.5, an older and smaller model that does use guidance (7 here) and a negative prompt. Here the terse prompt does produce the failures the story promises. Left to right: Level 0, Level 3 without a negative prompt, Level 3 with it. 512×512, seed 2026, 63 to 68 seconds each.

<Row>
<Figure id="anatomy-sd15-l0" alt="A basketball player in white dunking in the snow, one red shoe and one grey shoe, a pole standing alone beside him" caption="SD 1.5, Level 0: mismatched shoes, a pole that holds nothing, garbled jersey lettering." />
<Figure id="anatomy-sd15-l3" alt="A red-jersey player under a doubled hoop, an orange slab beneath the backboard" caption="SD 1.5, Level 3, no negative prompt: the hoop is doubled and melts into an orange slab." />
<Figure id="anatomy-sd15-l3neg" alt="A red-jersey player reaching for a single hoop mounted on a bent post, the shoes not matching" caption="SD 1.5, Level 3 + negative prompt: a single hoop, a strange post, shoes that still do not match." />
</Row>

The terse prompt shows a floating pole and mismatched shoes, and the detailed one shows a doubled hoop. With the negative prompt there is one hoop, and other oddities remain. **One image per prompt cannot tell you the negative prompt worked.** It is as likely to be the dice. If you want that claim, run it on twenty seeds and count; we did not.

## A checklist, and what the pictures say about it

Our working checklist for a prompt has five letters, S.A.C.L.N. It is a memory aid we use, not a result, and the table says what the pictures above do and do not support.

| | Say | In these pictures |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **S**ubject and anatomy | Who, attire, posture | Level 1 fixed the jersey and the pose. It also let the model choose the place. |
| **A**ction and motion | The trajectory, what touches what | Present from Level 0 ("dunking"); the ball above the hoop at Level 1 held. Stills cannot show motion. |
| **C**amera and optics | Focal length, aperture, framing | Level 3 changed the frame and softened the background. |
| **L**ighting and textures | Light source, materials | Not isolated: the light was already overcast at Level 2. We cannot judge textures from a handful of stills. |
| **N**egative shield | What to exclude | Not usable on FLUX.2 [klein] (guidance 1). Inconclusive on SD 1.5 with one seed. |

## What we did not measure

- **Variance.** One seed per prompt. A different seed could swap every conclusion about the doubled hoop or the shoes.
- **Quality, scored.** Our reading of the pictures is ours; the pictures and the prompts are yours to judge.
- **Video.** We have no naive-versus-detailed pair of clips, so we claim nothing about what a detailed prompt does for motion. A hosted clip costs $0.60 and we did not spend it for this.
- **An "Enhance prompt" button.** The idea that a product should write the detail for you is Orazaka's Studios, above. We do not describe any other feature here.
