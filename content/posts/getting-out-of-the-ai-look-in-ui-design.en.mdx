On October 20, I showed Krizaka UI to a few developers around me. The verdict came quickly: "It looks too AI. It's like every project generated by Claude: the same template, the same way of doing things, the same result at a glance."

It stung, because it was fair. The kit was written with an assistant, and it carried its signature. Each detail was defensible on its own. Together they made a set you have already seen a hundred times. Here is how we measured the problem, what we changed, and a checklist you can run on your own interface.

## Count first, judge later

Earlier that same day, I had already had three things removed that annoyed me: the cut-corner buttons, the blue, and the hovers. That was not the root of the problem. So before touching anything else I asked for an audit, measured in a real browser on the live sites (krizaka.com, ui.krizaka.com and the sites of two products).

Here is what the audit found.

The fonts were Inter, Plus Jakarta Sans at weight 800 and JetBrains Mono: the default trio of Next.js templates. The hero on krizaka.com was centred, with an uppercase mono label, a 64 px headline with an em-dash, one filled button and one ghost button. An uppercase mono label sat above 5 of 6 headings on krizaka.com and 6 of 7 on Orazaka and on Yomizaka (Yomizaka's home page had 25). A band of 3 to 4 big counting numbers followed every hero.

There were fake browser windows, with their three dots and address bar, on ui.krizaka.com, Orazaka and Yomizaka. There were blurred elements: 11 on krizaka.com, 6 on the Button page, 4 on the ui. home, 3 on Yomizaka. On the Button page alone, 231 elements had exactly the same 16 px radius.

The order of sections was the order of every SaaS page: label, heading, lead, grid of cards, call to action, FAQ. The icons had the 2 px rounded stroke you see everywhere. In the copy there were em-dashes in headlines and sentences built in threes. And the brand's birds were decoration, when they should have organised something.

One remark I find fair: the words themselves were specific, and they stayed. The problem was not the text. It was the packaging.

## Three directions, one winner

Rather than fix things at random, we drew three complete directions on the same real screens (the krizaka.com home, the Button page of ui., a Yomizaka lease check), light and dark, desktop and phone.

**Cahier** is the French school copybook, with its Seyès ruling, and a red margin where the section numbers go. The birds are drawn as naturalist plates with their promise as a caption. Blue ink on paper in light, chalk on slate in dark.

**Habitat** is Montréal's modular architecture: slabs with a solid side, no blur, products stacked on one another.

**Affiche** is a poster wall: flat colour blocks, headlines that change width, the birds as die-cut stickers.

I chose Cahier. The reason fits in a sentence: it is the only direction where the birds organise the system instead of decorating it. They become the plates and the captions, and a generic prompt does not produce that on its own. Habitat was the safe one, and the most likely to slide back into an ordinary developer tool. Affiche was the loudest, hard to keep calm on a business screen. From that one we keep a stamp for big moments.

## What changed, concretely

**The cut corners went.** Every call to action had a corner clipped at 45 degrees. A gimmick that made the most important control of a page look like a video game's. One 8 px radius, the radius of an input field, took its place. Then, with Cahier, 2 px on controls and 0 on cards.

**The blue.** The old one, `#196df5`, is a saturated electric blue: the blue of nearly every AI product. We drew five candidates as swatches, then on the real pages. The one we kept is a steel blue, `#3e76a8` in dark and `#2a5e8d` in light. Its hue (208) is exactly the complement of Orazaka's orange, which gives us a rule instead of a taste. White text on it reaches a contrast of 4.8 to 1 in dark and 6.7 to 1 in light, tested.

<Figure id="steel-before-after" alt="Four captures of the krizaka.com home page, before on top and after below: desktop dark, desktop light, phone dark, phone light. The electric blue on top, the steel blue below" caption="Before (top) and after (bottom): the blue and the buttons. The download figure differs between the two captures because it is read live." />

**No more light following the mouse, no more cards that lift.** On hover, a card rose 3 to 4 px with a coloured shadow, and a glow followed the pointer. A hover now changes a colour, never a position: the card's background goes up one step and its hairline strengthens, in 150 ms. A test moves a real pointer over each of the 754 Storybook stories and checks that nothing moves.

**No more blur.** Shadows are a solid offset, like a sheet laid a little to one side, never a halo. The bar is no longer frosted glass. I just recounted in a browser: on krizaka.com, no blurred element and no uppercase label, against 11 blurred elements at the start; on ui.krizaka.com, a single blur. The dominant radius is 2 px (70 elements on the krizaka.com home, 45 on ui.'s). Some pills and a few 12 and 14 px radii remain on krizaka.com: it is not finished.

**The numbers.** Our serif had a flaw: its default digits are old-style, with uneven heights, some rising and some dropping below the line. In the row « 4,514 », « 39 », « 19 », it danced. I saw it on krizaka.com, in French and in dark. The fix: lining, fixed-width digits wherever a figure is shown.

**One footer.** Another remark from the same developers: "the footer is too much, it looks like there are two." It was true: a block for the site, then four columns for the family, one of which listed only Krizaka. Now it is one band (Products, Open source, Krizaka), then one line for the signature, the version, Montréal and the legal links.

**Honest demo labels.** The plates on the products section show screens that work, but with sample data. Instead of invented figures that pass for real, each one says what it is: "Points per match day (demo league)", "Friends' league, match day 8 · demo", "quoted, page 3 · demo". And when a calculation appears, it is right: a salon's receipt shows $45 plus two taxes of $2.25 and $4.49, so $51.74, and a test checks the arithmetic to the cent.

## A checklist for your own interface

None of these rules is forbidden. It is their accumulation that makes the look. For each point, count, do not judge by eye.

1. **Fonts.** Open the inspector. If you find Inter, Plus Jakarta Sans or Poppins for the text, it is not a fault, but it is the first clue. Pick a font for a reason you can state.
2. **The hero.** Centred, gradient headline, two buttons? Try an asymmetric layout with one action.
3. **Uppercase mono labels above headings.** Count them. More than one in three is a tell.
4. **Fake browser windows.** Show the real component, labelled "demo data", or a captioned plate.
5. **Blurs.** Count the elements with a blur `filter` or `backdrop-filter`. Every halo should justify itself.
6. **Radii.** Count the distinct `border-radius` values and how they are spread. When 231 elements share one, it is not a system, it is a default.
7. **Section order.** If it can be guessed without reading (label, heading, three cards, call to action, FAQ), change at least one.
8. **Icons.** A 2 px rounded stroke everywhere is recognisable. Draw or choose a stroke with some character.
9. **Hovers.** A card that lifts and a glow that follows the mouse have become a template reflex. Changing a colour is enough.
10. **Copy.** Look for em-dashes and lists of three words. Read it aloud: if it sounds like a brochure, rewrite it.
11. **Invented figures.** Everything displayed is true, or says it is an example.

For those who would rather measure than guess, here is what to count in a browser console:

```js
const cs = [...document.querySelectorAll("body *")].map((e) => getComputedStyle(e));
({
  blurred: cs.filter((c) => /blur/.test(c.filter + c.backdropFilter)).length,
  uppercase: cs.filter((c) => c.textTransform === "uppercase").length,
  radii: Object.entries(
    cs.map((c) => c.borderTopLeftRadius).reduce((m, r) => ((m[r] = (m[r] || 0) + 1), m), {}),
  ).sort((a, b) => b[1] - a[1]),
});
```

## What I take from it

The AI look is not a flaw of the tool. It is what happens when nobody decided: every default is reasonable, and their sum is anonymous. What got us out was having numbers first, then several directions to compare on real screens, and finally an idea that starts from what we already have: birds that carry a promise.

Cahier can also turn twee if we are not careful. We keep the ruling for reading pages and showcases, never behind a dense application screen. The products' sites adopt the new style one by one. [Krizaka UI](https://ui.krizaka.com) already has it, and the decisions are written down in the [kit's repository](https://github.com/krizaka/krizaka-ui), with their tests.
