Most price pages for generative AI give you a rate and a promise. We would rather give you a measurement. This post comes from the records of [Orazaka](https://dev.orazaka.com), our engine: the credit ledger of real runs, and the rental ledger of the GPU machines behind them. It also says what we have **not** measured yet.

## How Orazaka meters a run

Every step of a run is metered, then billed in credits. A run takes **one hold** at the start, runs its steps, and **settles once** at the end; a generation that returns nothing releases its hold instead of being billed at a guess. In September we changed the unit: a credit went from 1 cent to 0.1 cent, so that small text steps stopped being rounded up to a minimum, and image generation, which had been billed at zero, became priced.

We then ran the same five workloads before and after, on the live database, and read the numbers from the hold and ledger tables.

## Five runs, before and after

| Studio | Debited, before → after (credits) | In cents | Change |
|:---|---:|---:|---:|
| trade-showcase | 3 → 121 | 3.00 → 12.10 ¢ | +303 % |
| lead-research | 3 → 14 | 3.00 → 1.40 ¢ | −53 % |
| outbound-prospection | 2 → 6 | 2.00 → 0.60 ¢ | −70 % |
| followup-sequences | 3 → 10 | 3.00 → 1.00 ¢ | −67 % |
| realestate-reels | 15 → 140 | 15.00 → 14.00 ¢ | −7 % |
| **Total** | **26 → 291** | **26.00 → 29.10 ¢** | **+12 %** |

The total hides two movements pulling in opposite directions, so read the rows.

- **Text and vision steps got 53 to 70 % cheaper.** Nothing was discounted: a charge that was only the minimum's padding stopped being made.
- **trade-showcase tripled** because one step, an image generation, used to be billed zero. At 512 × 512 and 20 steps it is **100 credits**, which is 10 cents, and the largest single line of that run. A capability that was free became priced.
- **realestate-reels barely moved** because 120 of its 140 credits are the composition of a 6-second video, already priced by its own rate.

<Note>
These are live models, not replays: the token counts differ between the before and after runs. Each row compares two samples of the same workload. The effect we measured is a factor of two to three; the sampling noise is a few percent.
</Note>

## What a run reserves, and what it costs

A hold is a safety margin, not a price. For these five Studios the reservations were 210, 380, 250, 510 and 6 400 credits, against 121, 14, 6, 10 and 140 actually debited. The long reservation of realestate-reels exists because its b-roll step, which contributed nothing in this run (the video backend answered HTTP 400), would cost about 4 500 credits when it succeeds.

## The hardware side

Our image and video work needs a GPU, and we do not keep one running. Orazaka's media workers run on a GPU machine created on demand and destroyed after 30 idle minutes. Our own ledger of the first four such sessions on 15 October records a machine rented at **$0.76 per hour**, for about **4.3 hours in total**, which is roughly **$3.24**. A fifth, larger machine was started after those sessions and is not counted here.

## What we have not measured

- **The cost of a generated video clip.** The 6 seconds above are the *assembly* of a reel; the b-roll step that generates clips did not run successfully in the sample. We will not quote a per-second price for generated video until we have a successful, measured run.
- **A cost per image across models and sizes.** We have one measured image configuration. Prices at other sizes are modelled, not measured.
- **Quality.** A cheaper step is only good news if its output is as good. We are not measuring that here.

When these numbers change, we will publish the new measurement next to this one.
