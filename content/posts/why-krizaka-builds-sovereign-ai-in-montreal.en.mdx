When we say **sovereign AI**, we mean something narrow and checkable: a customer can say where their documents are processed, which model read them, and who can see the result. Everything else on this page follows from that sentence.

## What "sovereign" means here

Three things, in the order we can prove them.

1. **A named place.** Our databases run in Toronto, Canada. The code that reads a document runs on infrastructure we rent and can name, not behind an opaque API we cannot inspect.
2. **A traced run.** Every analysis is a run of [Orazaka](https://dev.orazaka.com), our engine. The run keeps its identifier, the version of the Studio that ran, and the rules it applied. If a result is questioned, the run is the answer.
3. **No hidden model.** The model that did the work is a choice recorded in data, not a constant buried in a product. A product built on Orazaka never calls a model itself.

We do not claim more than that. Sovereign is not a synonym for "private by magic" or "better". It is a property you can verify, or it is marketing.

## Why Montréal

Krizaka is a company from Montréal, and two practical facts make the city a good place for this work: the law we read first is Québec's (the Civil Code, Law 25 on personal information), and the people who must trust the result live under it. A tool that reads a Québec lease should be built by people who have to live with the answer.

It also keeps us honest about language. Every Krizaka site ships in English and French from the first day, not as a later translation.

## What it constrains

Choosing to be checkable costs something, and we would rather list the costs than hide them.

- **We refuse a verdict out of nowhere.** A finding quotes the rule and the exact passage it rests on, or it does not appear.
- **We keep the work small.** No cluster we do not need, no service we cannot explain. Our product sites run on the smallest instances that work.
- **We publish the code.** The component kit ([Krizaka UI](https://ui.krizaka.com)) and the Spring Boot building blocks ([Krizaka Java](https://java.krizaka.com)) are open source, so the base of our products can be read by anyone.
- **We say what is not finished.** Several products are "in development": sign-up is closed, and their sites say so.

## What we have not solved

- Sovereignty of the *model* is still the hard part. Running a capable model on infrastructure we control costs real money and real GPU hours; we measure it in [the next post](/posts/measured-not-promised).
- Our Québec rules for lease checking still await a lawyer's review, and the reports say so.
- Rented infrastructure is not owned infrastructure. We know where our data sits; we do not build data centers.

## What to expect from this blog

Short posts, with the numbers we can point to and the runs that produced them. If a claim cannot be traced to a run, a measurement or a line of code, we will not write it.
