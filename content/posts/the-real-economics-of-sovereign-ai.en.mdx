Claims about the cost of generative AI are cheap; compute is not. Most platforms show a credit price and keep the real cost out of sight. Orazaka, our engine, records **how long each run took and what it cost**, and the public page [Measured runs](https://dev.orazaka.com/docs/measured-runs) is generated from those records, not typed. This post reads that page, adds the one calculation it enables, and says what it cannot tell you.

<Note>
Everything below is for one setup on 15 October 2026: a MacBook Pro (Apple M1 Max, 64 GB), DigitalOcean serverless inference, and rented GPUs. Times are measured from the command to the finished file. A figure that is derived, or comes from our working notes instead of the run records, is labelled as such.
</Note>

## What a run records

Each run of an Orazaka Studio keeps its start and finish, the output file (checked with `ffprobe`), the engine's own log line, and the price when a provider charges one. The clip below is one of those runs; open **How this was made** under it to see the model, the prompt, the seed, the time, the price and the run id you can look up.

## The measured table

Straight from the records of the Measured runs page (`docs/measurements/runs.json`, commit `dbe135b`):

| Model | Where it ran | Runs that finished | Time, command to file (median) | Price per output |
|:---|:---|---:|---:|---:|
| Wan 2.2 T2V A14B, 5 s clip, 832×480 | DigitalOcean serverless | 21 of 21 | 131 s (18 timed runs) | $0.60 |
| Stable Diffusion 3.5 Large, 1024×1024 | DigitalOcean serverless | 6 of 6 | 8 s | $0.08 |
| FLUX.2 [klein] 4B, 1024×1024 | The Mac | 6 of 18 | 62 s (engine 72.7 s) | none billed |
| Stable Diffusion 1.5, 512×512 | The Mac | 6 of 6 | 58 s | none billed |
| Z-Image Turbo, 1024×1024 | The Mac | 0 of 5 | engine 157.8 s | none billed |

Three things the table does not say by itself:

- **"None billed" is not "free".** We did not measure the Mac's electricity or its wear, so we do not put a number on them.
- **FLUX.2 [klein]'s 6 of 18 is mostly our plumbing.** The recorded causes are a client that gave up after about two minutes while the engine needed 60 to 75 s plus a 2.5 MB transfer, a deploy that restarted the relay, and a process mistake that overwrote two finished images. We count them anyway.
- **Three of the 21 clips took 264 s, 439 s and 1 063 s**, because the Mac worker had lost its tunnel and the jobs waited in the queue. They are not in the median, and they are in the table's 21.

## Three ways to run the same work

Orazaka does not care where a model runs; it routes. That makes three archetypes comparable on one scale.

1. **Your own hardware.** Open-weight models on the machine you already have. On the Mac that is one image a minute and no bill. The limit is memory: Wan 2.2 TI2V 5B ran out of memory while decoding a 480×832 clip on the 64 GB machine. For data that must not leave the premises, this is the tier where the question "who read it?" has one answer.
2. **A hosted API, pay per output.** Eight seconds and eight cents for a 1024×1024 image; 131 seconds and sixty cents for a five-second clip. Nothing to run, nothing idle, and the price is a fixed number you can check in the table.
3. **A rented GPU, pay per hour.** Our ledger of four DigitalOcean RTX 4000 Ada sessions on 15 October adds up to **4.26 hours, or $3.24**, at $0.76 per hour; Orazaka's watchdog destroys the machine after 30 idle minutes, because an idle GPU costs the same as a busy one.

Routing between them is a data question, not code: which model, where, in what order lives in the database (a rule we apply across the platform), so adding an overflow to a hosted API is a row, not a release.

## When does a rented GPU beat the hosted price?

This is the one calculation the table makes possible. A rented GPU's price per clip is its hourly rate divided by the clips it makes in an hour **while busy**, divided again by how busy it actually is. Our RunPod H100 note gives 167 s of generation for a 1280×704 Wan 2.2 TI2V 5B clip at $3.99 per hour. Move the slider to see where it crosses the hosted $0.60.

<Utilization hourly={3.99} seconds={167} hosted={0.6} />

Read it carefully:

- **It is derived, not measured.** The 167 s and the $3.99 come from our working notes, not from `runs.json`. Boot time, the weights download and idle minutes are not counted as work, which is exactly why the real number is higher.
- **The two clips are not the same.** The hosted clip is 832×480; the rented one is 1280×704. A fair comparison needs the same clip on both, and we have not run it.
- **What it does show** is the shape of the answer. At full load the rented GPU is about a third of the hosted price; as it idles, the advantage disappears and then reverses. That is the whole case for "zero idle": create the machine when there is work and destroy it when there is not.

## What we do not claim

A gross-margin figure is the number everyone wants from a post like this. We do not give one: our records hold the **cost** of each run, not the revenue, so a margin cannot be computed from them, and an unmeasured margin is just a number. What we can show is the mechanism that keeps a price tied to cost. Orazaka reserves an estimate before a run (a hold), then settles against what the run actually consumed, and a generation that returns nothing releases its hold instead of being billed at a guess ([ADR-033](https://github.com/krizaka/orazaka)). How that played out on five real workloads, before and after we changed the unit of a credit, is in [Measured, not promised](/posts/measured-not-promised).

Also not measured:

- The Mac's electricity and wear.
- The cost of a clip on the hourly RTX 4000 Ada: we have its hours and dollars, not a clip time.
- Quality. The images and clips in these posts are there for you to judge; we measured seconds and dollars.

The sibling post, [What it really costs to generate images and video](/posts/what-image-and-video-generation-costs), puts the same images side by side and lists the limits we hit. When the setup changes we measure again and publish the new numbers next to these.
