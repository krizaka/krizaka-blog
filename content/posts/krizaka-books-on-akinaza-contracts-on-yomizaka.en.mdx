Before offering a tool to people who have real clients and real deadlines, I wanted to ask it a simple question: would we, Krizaka, use it? Not in a demo. For our own invoices and our own contracts.

A word of honesty first. Everything you see in this post is demonstration: fictional clients, plausible amounts, a development environment. The document templates are Krizaka's, but we removed everything that identifies the company before sending them anywhere: no company number, no address, no bank details.

## The books, in Akinaza

Krizaka is a small software studio in Montréal. It mostly sells hours, and a few flat fees. Éric, who runs it in our demo, wanted one place for three things: the services he bills, invoices his clients read in French or in English, and the sales-tax return prepared without a spreadsheet.

<Clip id="akinaza-books-en" alt="In Akinaza, a studio's list of invoices: each card gives the demo client, the document's language, the date, the due date, the subtotal, the two taxes and the total" caption="The studio's invoices in Akinaza. Screen recording on our development environment, demonstration data." />

Each service has a name in both languages, a price and a tax category. An invoice comes out of those services, in the client's language, with the taxes computed once on the taxable total, to the cent. It carries the supplier's tax numbers and its payment terms with the due date: what a buyer needs to claim its credits.

Each invoice posts its taxes to the books in the same step, and the database refuses an unbalanced entry. I care about that rule more than any feature: a book that does not balance is not a book.

And the period's return is prepared from those books, line by line, with the documents behind each figure. Prepared, never filed: Éric reads it and files it himself. Akinaza sends nothing to anyone. That is a choice, not an oversight.

## The contracts and invoices, in Yomizaka

Before Krizaka became Akinaza's first client, its invoice and service-contract templates went through Yomizaka. Here is the video of the walk-through, sped up where there is waiting.

<Clip id="yomizaka-review-en" alt="In Yomizaka, a services contract dropped on the left and, on the right, the report: four rules evaluated, no blocking failure, each rule with its article of law and the run's identifier" caption="Krizaka's services contract checked in Yomizaka. Screen recording, redacted copy, development environment." />

The invoice: eight rules, eight passed, each with the passage of the text that supports it and the article of law behind it: the date, the tax numbers, the total, the taxes, the payment terms. The contract: four rules, four passed, on late interest, copyright, moral rights and the signature. The report itself says what it does not cover: the Civil Code of Québec, to be reviewed by a lawyer.

Each check is a traced run of Orazaka, the engine working behind. Its identifier is on the screen, so you can find it again.

## What the failures taught us

This is the part that helped me most, and it is not flattering.

The first try, made directly on the engine with the invoice's raw text, failed on two rules: the total and the taxes. Yet both lines were there. The engine did not find them when an amount was separated from its label by a long run of spaces, or when a calculation in brackets came in between. It was not a mistake in the invoice, it was a limit of the checker. In Yomizaka, which normalises spaces, the same template passes eight of eight. We opened [a ticket on the rules side](https://github.com/krizaka/orazaka-packs/issues/81), and it is now closed.

The second is the other way round. We ran in Yomizaka an invoice that came out of Akinaza itself. It failed: the payment terms were missing. A billing product whose own invoice is rejected by another of our products is humbling, and it is exactly why you close the loop. The [ticket](https://github.com/krizaka/akinaza/issues/113) is closed: the invoice now carries its due date and its terms.

As for the contract, it passes four of four for a reason that bothers me a little: it assigns no right, so there is nothing to object to. Silence passes. What no rule reads, for example a late-interest rate stated two different ways between the contract and the invoice, I found by reading, and noted for the person who has to decide. Yomizaka helps, it does not replace a lawyer. It says so on every screen.

## What remains to be done

The hand-off from one tool to the other is manual today: it is the checked text that we print from Akinaza. An import through the API, which would turn a template checked in Yomizaka into an Akinaza template, is [proposed](https://github.com/krizaka/akinaza/issues/115), not built.

## What you can take from it

If you build a tool for others, use it on yourself first, with your real documents (redacted) and not polished examples. The two failures in this post happened because we looked at our own documents, and no demonstration would have shown them. And always keep, somewhere, an honest sentence about what the tool does not do.
