A tool is judged by what it leaves on the screen. Since October, in Verikora, Folioviv and Sovkora, we have been removing from our screens what did not serve the person looking at them. Each choice is written down in a decision record next to the code: the problem, the principle behind it, what we refused, what it costs, and what would tell us we are wrong.

Here are five of those choices. Each one is shown on a real screen of our development builds, with an invented persona from our demo cast, never a customer and never an administrator. For each, the reason comes first, then the cost. When we have a measure, we give it. When a screen does not follow its own rule yet, we say so at the end.

## One primary action when you arrive

Éric Tremblay opens Verikora with a lease in PDF. He wants to know what is questionable in it, and he does not want to look for where to drop it.

<Figure id="ergo-verikora-home" alt="Verikora home: one filled button, Check a document, in the header." caption="Verikora, the home, Éric Tremblay (demo), 1440 px, dark theme. One filled button: « Vérifier un document » (Check a document)." />

The home has one primary action, the one in the header. There is no dashed drop zone in the middle of the page any more: the whole window takes the file. Éric can drop his PDF anywhere and the check starts. Behind the primary action there is still a real file input, for the keyboard and for phones, where nobody drags a file. This is what [the drop overlay](site:ui/components/drop-overlay) of our component library does.

Measured on the home on 15 November: one filled button, the header's.

**Why.** The first version of this home had a drop zone and two checkboxes that sat on three different screens, and the thing to do came fourth. The principle we wrote down is « the answer first; one drop zone per screen, the window itself elsewhere ». It is the same idea as [recognition rather than recall](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ten-usability-heuristics/): the person should not have to hunt for the one thing the page is for. On 4 November we removed the dashed zone, its second primary button and the « Or try » suggestions from the home. Only the very first-day screen keeps them, because a person with nothing yet needs an example.

What we refused:

- A « Load the demo » button, because no service did it. It would have promised something the product does not do.
- Starting the analysis when we do not know which document it is, because a wrong file still costs an analysis. Verikora asks one question instead, « Which document? ».

The cost: a dropped file takes the owner's default options, since the options only make sense once the files are known.

In Sovkora the same idea takes another shape, because the screen is a list. Each Studio has one action on its row, « Run » when it is ready, « Get the pack » otherwise, and its state in words.

<Figure id="ergo-sovkora-studios" alt="Sovkora Studios list: each row has its state and one action, Run." caption="Sovkora, Studios, persona « Krizaka » (demo), 1440 px, dark theme. A filter « All 45 · Ready 29 · To get 16 », one action per row." />

Before, it was a two-column grid of cards, three per screen, and nobody could tell an installed Studio from an available one, or one that needs a pack. We wanted each row to say what you get and what it costs. We refused two tabs, « Ready » and « To get »: the same Studio would have lived in two places depending on what you own. The filter lives in the address, so a filtered list can be sent as a link. The cost: the description is one truncated line per row.

## On a phone, nothing spills over and everything is under the thumb

The same Éric reads a check standing in a building's stairwell, his phone in one hand.

<Figure id="ergo-verikora-phone" alt="Verikora on a phone: the checks list as rows in dark theme, and the settings as one menu in light theme." caption="Verikora, 390 px, Éric Tremblay (demo). Left, « Vérifications » in dark: a title, one line of facts, the result at the end. Right, « Réglages » in light: the sections become a menu." />

On a phone, a Verikora list is no longer a table. Each row carries a title, one line of facts (« Bail · 1 nov. 2026 », a lease and its day) and the result at the end. The rest waits in the detail, one tap away. On wider screens the table stays. The settings had five tabs in a row, which pushed the page 167 px past a 390 px screen, and 197 px at 360 px. On a phone those sections are now a single menu.

Measured on 15 November: none of the nine screens we captured scrolls sideways at 390 px, in light or dark (0 px).

**Why.** Two reasons, one for each fix. A page that needs a sideways scroll at phone width breaks [the reflow rule of WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/reflow.html): content should fit the width without scrolling in two directions. And a row that prints four « label: value » pairs per document makes the person read a form, when they came for a title and a result. So the row keeps a title, one line of facts and the result, and the rest is one tap away. The cost: a phone shows two lines per row, not the whole record.

The phone also changes the order of the blocks. On a computer, « To review » fills the right third of the home. On a phone it comes right after the applicant files. For Éric, who has none, it is the first block under the title.

The Sovkora phone app made the same call for navigation (we have no screenshot of it here, only its decision record): four tabs at the bottom, under the thumb, each with its icon and a word that is never cut. We refused the hidden menu at the top, because it made the most-used places the hardest to reach with one hand. The reasons we wrote down are that most people hold a phone in one hand and reach the lower half of the screen, that [a visible bar beats a hidden menu for discoverability](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/hamburger-menus/), and that [an icon with its label](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/icon-usability/) is clearer than an icon alone. The cost: the plan, the packs and the credits are not on the phone. Whoever wants them goes to the web.

## An empty screen says what to do

Éric creates a collection « À classer » (to sort) and opens it before putting anything in.

<Figure id="ergo-verikora-empty" alt="Verikora, empty collection: the owl, one sentence and a drop zone; below, a screen offering Change role." caption="Verikora, Éric Tremblay (demo), 1440 px. Top, dark: the empty collection. Bottom, light: « Applicant files » seen by a tenant." />

The empty screen does not stay blank. It shows the owl, one sentence (« Rien ici pour l'instant. Déposez un document ou créez un dossier. », nothing here yet, drop a document or create a folder) and the only drop zone on the screen, with what it accepts: PDF, Word, OpenDocument, text or photo, up to 10 MB. On the first day, the whole empty library adds two examples to try, a tenant's lease and a landlord's file.

When a page is not meant for you, it says so instead of looking broken: « Les dossiers de candidats sont pour les gestionnaires » (applicant files are for property managers), then one button, « Changer de rôle » (change role).

**Why.** [An empty state is the product's chance to teach](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/empty-state-interface-design/): it should say what the place is for and what to do first. The rules live in the library, so every product follows them: [the empty state](site:ui/components/empty-state) has a title that says what is empty and a description that says what will fill it, with one action, two at most. The mascot never carries the message alone ([the mascot state](site:ui/components/mascot-state) is decoration around words). The point is the number of gestures: one tap from the empty screen to the first file, or none if you drop the document on the page.

<Figure id="ergo-folioviv-empty-phone" alt="Folioviv on a phone: My bookings is empty; the beaver and one sentence say what will show here." caption="Folioviv, « Mes réservations » (My bookings), Éric Tremblay (demo, client side), 390 px, dark theme." />

Folioviv follows the same rule with its beaver. « Aucune réservation pour l'instant » (no bookings yet), then what will come: your bookings, your invoices and your files.

## An error stays in place and offers the next move

Éric has uploaded a maintenance contract. The PDF is built in a way Verikora cannot read.

<Figure id="ergo-verikora-failed" alt="Verikora, failed check: the message in plain words, then Try again and Choose another file, on desktop and phone." caption="Verikora, a check that did not finish, Éric Tremblay (demo). Left at 1440 px in dark, right at 390 px in light." />

The message sits where the report would have been. It says what happened in our words, with no engine name: « Ce PDF n'est pas sous une forme que nous pouvons vérifier. Choisissez une autre copie du document, ou réessayez. » (this PDF is not in a form we can check; choose another copy of the document, or try again). Two moves follow, « Réessayer » and « Choisir un autre fichier ». One tap leads to the upload page. The menu on the left and the bar at the top stay usable, and the technical message stays in our logs. When a PDF has no text, the message also says what still runs: « L'examen du fichier, lui, a lieu. » (the examination of the file itself does take place).

**Why.** [WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/error-suggestion.html) asks that when an error is detected and we know how to fix it, the suggestion is given to the person. We went one step further and decided that the error and its next move belong on the same screen, in the place of the result that did not come. [Alert](site:ui/components/alert) is the component that carries it. That is why we refused two things:

- The notification that fades after a few seconds, for a failure the person must act on. They may not even be looking.
- A single loading screen shared by every page. It is only a centred spinner under another name. Each page has its own [skeleton](site:ui/components/skeleton), in its own shape, shown only after 300 ms so a fast page never flickers. The only spinner in Verikora belongs to a button that is working, and a test refuses every other one.

The cost: thirty-eight small files, two per page, and a new page has to bring its two or its test fails. The signal that would tell us we are wrong is people retrying a page that keeps failing, because the cause is not theirs. Then the error needs a status link.

## Two themes, and nothing moves if you asked for it

<Figure id="ergo-verikora-themes" alt="Verikora appearance settings: System, Light and Dark, shown in dark then light." caption="Verikora, Settings, Appearance, Éric Tremblay (demo), 1440 px. Top, the dark theme selected; bottom, light." />

Every screen exists in light and dark. The choice is in the settings: « Système » follows the device, « Clair », or « Sombre », which is the default. The choice is kept in the browser. The colours come from [one set of tokens](site:ui/foundations/tokens) that both themes read, and [the theming page](site:ui/theming) says how a product wears its own colour. [prefers-color-scheme](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/Reference/At-rules/@media/prefers-color-scheme) is what lets « System » follow the device.

Motion follows the same logic. When your device asks for less motion, nothing moves. We measured it on the nine screens we captured: no running animation with reduced motion. With motion allowed, we count up to 11 on the settings and 6 on the Studios list.

**Why.** [prefers-reduced-motion](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/Reference/At-rules/@media/prefers-reduced-motion) exists because motion can make some people ill, and [WCAG 2.2 asks](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/animation-from-interactions.html) that motion triggered by an interaction can be switched off. Our rule is [one motion system](site:ui/foundations/motion): an animation is there because it shows something, never to decorate, and it stops under reduced motion. In the library, an empty screen's bird arrives once, then the words, then the action, and not at all under reduced motion. Every stage of a check is a word too, so reduced motion loses nothing.

## What we have not fixed yet

On the failed-check screen, under « Réessayer », the « Au regard de la loi » card keeps a filled « Lancer la vérification » button. Two filled buttons on one screen: what we refuse elsewhere.

Our rule also asks for targets of at least 44 px on a phone, and we do not hold it everywhere. That is stricter than [WCAG 2.2's minimum of 24 px](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/target-size-minimum.html), and still short of what we ask. Measured at 390 px, « Réessayer » is 40 px tall and « Choisir des fichiers » 36 px. The buttons that expand a row of the checks list are 28 × 28 px, and their name for screen readers is still in English.

In Folioviv, the empty bookings screen says to open a shop's page, but its only button opens *your own* shop.

These gaps are logged. If a screen of ours makes you hesitate, write to us: most of these choices came from there.

## What we measured, and what we did not

The screens were captured on 15 November, on our development builds, with Playwright, at 1440 and 390 px, in light and dark, with reduced motion, and a second pass with motion allowed. We counted horizontal overflow, controls under 44 px, running animations and filled buttons. The « before » figures (167 px, three screens, four pairs per row) are the ones written in our records on the day of each decision; we did not measure them again. We timed nothing and asked no real person: what a choice saves is counted in gestures and in gaps to a rule, never in minutes.
